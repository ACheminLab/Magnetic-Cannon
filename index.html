The magnetic cannon

A steel ball gains speed as it is attracted to a permanent magnet. A nonlinear compression wave then carries energy through the chain, ejecting the ball at the far end. Explore how magnetic energy, chain length and transmission losses shape the outcome.

Based on Chemin et al. · AJP 85, 495 (2017)

Model and interpretation

The attraction phase adds magnetic potential energy to the incoming ball. A collision launches a strongly nonlinear compression pulse through the touching spheres. The ejected ball must also escape the magnetic attraction on the far side.

Kimpact = Kinit + Un
γ(N) = max(0, γ₀ − 0.024N)
Kfinal = max[0, γ(N)Kimpact − Um−1]

The full article derives the magnetic force from induced magnetization and models ball-to-ball contacts using Hertz's law. This teaching view compresses the collision details into an experimentally motivated energy-transmission factor.

Reference: A. Chemin, P. Besserve, A. Caussarieu, N. Taberlet, and N. Plihon, “Magnetic cannon: The physics of the Gauss rifle,” American Journal of Physics 85, 495–502 (2017). doi:10.1119/1.4979653.
